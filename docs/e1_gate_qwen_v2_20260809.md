### 1.2 CHANGE 1 — band-targeted exemplar selection

`src/judex_calibration/control_fewshot.py`. Within each (subject, letter) bucket the
candidate pool is restricted to rows whose `answer_justification` length is in
**[400, 800] characters** before the existing least-used-rater walk runs. Deterministic;
coverage-complete; documented nearest-the-band fallback when a bucket holds no in-band row
(never a coverage cost — only a genuinely empty bucket is an error). Recorded as
`exemplar_selection: "band_400_800"` in the leg meta sidecar, with an explicit
resume-guard clause that fires **even when the prior sidecar omits the key** — which is
exactly the Phase-2c case, so a Phase-2c run dir cannot be continued under this harness.

Why: store v2 fixed the floor and introduced a ceiling. Its spans run to 1509 characters,
and the Mac smoke ladder (qwen3-4b-base, 13 items, greedy, v2 store throughout) measured
what a length-blind draw costs:

| iteration | selection | exemplar spans | parse rate |
|---|---|---|---:|
| it0 | v1 store, length-blind | 103–404 | 0.846 |
| it1 | v2 store, length-blind | 648–1509 | **0.538** (worst) |
| it2 | v2 store, **band** | 507–781 | **0.846** (best) |
| it3 | v2 store, band + TeX-hardened prompt | 507–781 | 0.692 |

Zero empty-reasoning cells in every v2 iteration — the length-blind draw's extra failures
were JSON-breaking output, i.e. models imitating elaborateness into illegal escapes and
raw control characters.

### 1.3 CHANGE 2 — last-object parsing on the raw control path

`src/judex_calibration/elicit_control.py`: `elicit_cell` now parses stage 2 with
`parse_last_control` (the chat path's semantics) instead of the first-`{` scan. Record
shape unchanged.

Why: Phase 2c's Mode A had two sub-causes and only one was the store's. MMLU stems are
full of TeX, so a model that restates `\frac{x}{12}` before its JSON hands the first-object
scanner `{x}` — balanced, not the answer, and a `json.loads` failure. No store content can
fix that; the parser has to skip non-answer objects.

Two things to read off this table.

**`high_school_mathematics` is repaired: 10/20 → 19/20.** It was the worst subject for
every Phase-2c base leg. Degenerate reasoning spans there fell from 6 to 2, and — the part
that matters — *the two that remain still parse*. Across the whole leg, three cells emitted
a ≤ 1-character reasoning span and **all three produced valid JSON anyway** (Phase 2c: six
such cells, three of which failed). The degenerate span has stopped being fatal.

**`econometrics` is now the sole binding subject**, unchanged at 16/20, and all four of its
failures are `Invalid \escape`. This is the boundary store v2 was explicitly documented as
*not* being able to cross, and the run confirms the documentation is honest rather than
conservative — but it also refines it. Only 2 of the 4 failing econometrics stems contain a
backslash at all:

| subject | backslash-bearing stems / 20 | escape failures |
|---|---:|---:|
| high_school_mathematics | 5 | 0 |
| econometrics | 2 | 4 |
| all others | 0 | 0 |

So this is not simple imitation of TeX in the prompt: on the subject with the *most*
TeX-bearing stems the escape mode is now extinct, while on econometrics the model
introduces LaTeX-shaped statistical notation of its own accord. The residue is a
**domain-notation** effect, not a scaffold effect, and no exemplar rewrite reaches it.
At 4/20 it costs ~3.3 points of leg-level parse rate — affordable against a 0.90 gate, and
the reason the gate still clears with 5 points to spare.

**Caveat that the GO recommendation has to carry.** Mode B (the empty-reasoning collapse
that produced 62/76 of gemma31's failures, 53/60 of glm's and 18/25 of maverick's) was
**never qwen's mode** — 0/16 in Phase 2c and 0/6 here. This leg therefore does **not**
directly test the fix for the mode that broke the other three families. What it tests is
that the span-matched store and the band draw do not *cost* anything, plus the partial
signal above (degenerate spans halved, and no longer fatal). See §7.

## 4. Reasoning-span distribution vs the exemplar band

Exemplar spans actually shown, all six subjects (k=4 each, 24 exemplars): **637–798
characters, median 723, zero out of band.**

Model-produced reasoning spans, characters:

| leg | n | p25 | median | p75 | max | in band [400, 800] |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Phase 2c passing cells | 104 | 285 | 480 | 1364 | 10213 | 18.3% |
| **this run, passing cells** | 114 | **614** | **793** | 1148 | 8788 | **43.9%** |
| this run, failing cells | 6 | 678 | 884 | 2594 | 4943 | 33.3% |

The band draw moved the model's own output onto the scaffold: the median passing span is
**793 characters against an exemplar median of 723** — the model writes almost exactly as
much as it is shown — and the in-band fraction more than doubles (18.3% → 43.9%) while the
lower quartile rises from 285 to 614. The interquartile range narrows from 1079 to 534
characters.

The failing cells sit in the **right** tail, not the left: their p75 is 2594 characters
against 1148 for passing cells, and their mean is 1742 against 1055. That is the
elaborateness mechanism the Mac smoke ladder measured, observed here at full scale — what
breaks the JSON is writing *too much*, not too little. It is also why a band with an upper
bound is the right instrument rather than a floor alone.

## 7. Verdict for the remaining campaign — **GO**

**GO** on the four remaining legs (gemma31 base 1×H200 raw, glm base 8×H200, maverick base
8×H200, qwen post 1×H200), est. $55–65.

What the run established:

1. **The gate clears on a real base checkpoint**, with 5 points of margin, on the family
   that came closest in Phase 2c. This is the first base leg in the programme to do so.
2. **CHANGE 2 removed a whole failure class.** The six `Expecting property name` cells —
   the brace-hijack signature — are gone, and no cell in this leg failed that way. That
   class was 37.5% of Phase 2c's qwen-pre failures and it cost nothing to remove: the
   parser is unit-tested inert on single-object and no-object emissions.
3. **CHANGE 1 demonstrably steers the model's span** onto the exemplar band (median 793 vs
   exemplar median 723; in-band fraction 18.3% → 43.9%), and the failures that remain are
   in the long right tail, exactly as the smoke ladder predicted.
4. **The residual is characterised and bounded.** One subject, one mechanism
   (domain-notation LaTeX in econometrics), ~3.3 points of leg-level parse rate, not
   reachable by any further exemplar work.

What it did **not** establish, and what the remaining legs are therefore still exposed to:

- **Mode B is untested by this leg.** qwen never had it. gemma31 (36.7%), glm (50.0%) and
  maverick (79.2%) failed Phase 2c mostly *through* it, and their repair rests on the store
  v2 span floor plus the band draw — supported here only indirectly (degenerate spans
  halved, and the survivors now parse). **gemma31 base should be run FIRST of the four**:
  it is a 1×H200 leg (~$1), it was the *worst* Phase-2c leg at 36.7%, and it was the family
  the empty-reasoning probe was run against. If gemma31 base clears, Mode B is repaired and
  the two 8×H200 legs (~$25–28 each, the bulk of the budget) are justified. If it does not,
  stop there having spent ~$1 rather than ~$55.
- **qwen post's own gate failure is a different object.** It failed at 81.67% on JSON
  syntax, and this run repairs the base leg only. It should be expected to improve — the
  brace-hijack class is gone harness-wide and the store is math-safe — but it is not
  evidence-backed by this leg and should not be assumed.
- **Cost per leg is up**, for the good reason in §6. The 8×H200 legs will run longer than
  Phase 2c's did; the $55–65 estimate should be treated as the low end.

Recommended order: **gemma31 base → qwen post → glm base → maverick base**, with a
stop-and-report after gemma31 base.
